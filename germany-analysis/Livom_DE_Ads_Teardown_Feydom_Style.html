01 — Positioning

One idea does almost all the work: "the sofa adapts to you, not the other way around."

The verb anpassen / anpasst ("to adapt") appears roughly 149 times across the corpus — the single most repeated concept in their entire ad library. Every seemingly different ad — pets, kids, moving house, stained cushions, decor-indecision jokes — is the same repositioning line with a different life scenario plugged in.

The core line
"Weil dein Sofa sich anpassen soll, nicht du."Because your sofa should adapt — not you.
The reframe
"Warum nicht ein Sofa, das sich deinem Leben anpasst – statt umgekehrt?"Why not a sofa that adapts to your life — instead of the other way around?
The permission
"Kinder, Haustiere, Snacks auf der Couch? Gar kein Problem."Kids, pets, snacks on the couch? No problem at all.

03 — Promotional Cadence

Not always on sale — brand-building first, then permanently on sale

Checked every day of coverage rather than assuming: Livom ran zero discount-percentage ads for the first ~7 months in this dataset. Discounting only starts in November 2025 — then it never stops.

What this actually means

Livom spent roughly its first 7 months of paid social (through late 2025) building the "adapts to you" brand line with zero discounting. Once the discount machine switched on in November 2025, coverage became continuous — no gap longer than the overlap of consecutive sales — through at least July 2026. If you're earlier in your own brand's lifecycle, this suggests a sequencing worth copying: establish the positioning first, then layer in permanent, rotating urgency once the message is proven.

Inside the 94-variant evergreen ad, the text never changes. What varies: the video edit (81 distinct video files across 90 video-format ads), the CTA button (75× "Learn more" vs 19× "Shop now"), and the landing page (72× homepage, 20× the modular-sofas collection page). Targeting differences are invisible to us — Meta's Ad Library doesn't expose that data.

The relaunch pattern

A video is never paired with more than one script — checked every asset, zero exceptions. Instead, the same winning video+text pair gets resubmitted as a brand-new ad unit every 3–9 weeks for up to 10 months straight, almost certainly to reset Meta's frequency/fatigue penalties rather than to test something new.

08 — So What Should You Do

Playbook notes for entering the German market

1. Pick one repositioning line, repeat relentlessly

Livom's library boils down to one sentence reused ~150 times. Find your version of "adapts to you, not the other way around" and let every ad be a variation on it.

2. Lead with risk-reversal, not craftsmanship

Trial period, free returns, free delivery/assembly should dominate copy 10-15× more than quality/heritage claims — that's the real objection for online furniture buyers.

3. Build a UGC bench, not one-off posts

Recruit rotating creators with a repeatable script (problem → reveal → personal code → risk-reversal). Reuse each creator's code across multiple pieces for cleaner attribution.

4. Establish the message first, discount second

Livom ran ~7 months of brand-only ads before ever discounting. Once it started, it never stopped — but positioning came first.

5. Rotate winning creative, don't "set and forget"

Relaunch best-performing video/text pairs as fresh ad units every 4-8 weeks to dodge frequency fatigue.

6. Expect ~40% of new creative to die within a month

Budget for aggressive testing and fast kill decisions — only 3-4 concepts out of 82 ever scaled past 10 variants.
